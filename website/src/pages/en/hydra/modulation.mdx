# Modulation

Modulation is one of the most powerful techniques in Hydra. It uses the colors of one source to **distort the geometry** of another.

## How modulation works

When modulating:
- The modulator's red channel → offset along X
- The modulator's green channel → offset along Y
- Bright areas are distorted more

---

## modulate() — Basic modulation

```javascript
source.modulate(texture, amount)
```

  client:only="react"

  .out()
`}

## Complex examples

### Layered modulation

<MiniRepl
  client:only="react"
  tune={`await initHydra()
osc(10, 0.1, 0.5)
  .modulate(noise(3), 0.2)
  .modulateRotate(osc(2), 0.5)
  .modulateScale(voronoi(5), 0.3)
  .out()
`}
/>

### Self-modulation (feedback)

<MiniRepl
  client:only="react"
  tune={`await initHydra()
osc(10, 0.1, 0.5)
  .modulate(o0, 0.1)
  .out()
`}
/>

### Cross modulation

<MiniRepl
  client:only="react"
  tune={`await initHydra()
osc(10, 0.1, 0.5).modulate(o1, 0.2).out(o0)
noise(3).modulate(o0, 0.2).out(o1)
render(o0)
`}
/>

### Combining with color

<MiniRepl
  client:only="react"
  tune={`await initHydra()
osc(20, 0.03, 0)
  .modulate(noise(3), () => Math.sin(time) * 0.3)
  .color(1, 0.5, 0.2)
  .kaleid(4)
  .out()
`}
/>

---

## Modulation tips

1. **Start with small values** (0.1-0.3)
2. **Use noise() as the modulator** for organic distortions
3. **Combine with color effects**
4. **Animate the intensity**: `() => Math.sin(time) * 0.3`
