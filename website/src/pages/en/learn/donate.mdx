# Support the work

I build free apps that run on your own computer: music, dubbing, voice and more. All the source code is open and lives on GitHub.

## Why it matters

Without support, the apps won't vanish overnight, but they'll slowly fade: fewer updates, bugs that linger, new models arriving late. With support, it's the other way round. Which of the two happens is up to you.

## Who's behind this

<img src="/donate-img/author.webp" alt="Nerual Dreming" width="250" style="float:left;margin:4px 24px 12px 0;border-radius:12px" />

I'm not a famous blogger or a hot startup founder. No megacorporation funds me, I don't live off patents or rent — I just love neural networks and vibe coding. For twenty years I led the creation of products as a product and project manager: I came up with features, set tasks and reviewed them for teams of dozens of people. Now, with AI, I bring my ideas to life on my own and share them with the world — with the people who need them and whom they help.

My apps are often one of a kind: the newest AI models in an interface I design and refine myself. My principle is to squeeze the best possible speed and efficiency out of them: I test the models and check the benchmarks myself. The apps are easy to install and use, bugs get fixed within a few releases, and releases come out almost every week. That's what software should be like, in my view, and not every paid app reaches that level.

I don't want premium versions or a split into paying and non-paying users. I want as many people as possible to have access to the newest AI, so everything I make is cross-platform and runs on as much hardware as I can manage: older graphics cards, plain CPUs, Linux, macOS. It takes time, but it's worth it.

## Questions

<details>
<summary>Do I need a crypto wallet?</summary>

No, a card is enough. You never hold any crypto yourself: the service delivers it to me. A wallet is only needed to send crypto you already own.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is it safe?</summary>

Yes. You pay on the pages of the payment services themselves, and your card details go only to them. I never see your card or your personal data.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is it anonymous? Why do they ask for ID?</summary>

Exchange services are regulated, so the first time they may ask for your email and a quick ID check. That stays with them, nothing is passed to me. Some skip the check for small amounts. On dalink you can donate as «Anonymous», and a crypto transfer from your own wallet doesn't reveal your name to me.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How will you know the donation was from me?</summary>

On dalink and Boosty I see the name and message you leave. Crypto arrives without a name, so if you'd like me to know who it's from, use dalink or Boosty.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do I get anything in return?</summary>

My gratitude. Support doesn't buy priority, a feature guarantee or special treatment: it pays for the work, not for a place in the queue. Boosty subscribers also get the perks of their tier, listed on the Boosty page.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Something went wrong. What should I do?</summary>

If a payment didn't go through, contact that service's support: only they can see your payment. If something is wrong with one of my apps, open an issue on [GitHub](https://github.com/timoncool) or send a report right from the app.

</details>
